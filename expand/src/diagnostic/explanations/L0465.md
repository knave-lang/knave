A `rust-trait` lists in its `(requires ...)` clause the trait it declares, or a
trait that requires the declared trait, directly or through other traits. So
`(requires lower-hex)` in the `rust-trait` of `lower-hex` reports it, and so do
two traits that require each other. The error marks the requirement in the trait
of the loop that the program declares last, and the other traits of that clause
stay required. A `deftrait` with a `rust` clause writes a `rust-trait`, so its
`requires` clause reports it too.

## Erroneous example

```lisp
(rust-trait lower-hex "std::fmt::LowerHex"
  (requires upper-hex)
  i64)

(rust-trait upper-hex "std::fmt::UpperHex"
  (requires lower-hex)
  i64)

(def hex
  (rust "format!(\"{{:x}}\", {0})"
    (where ((a lower-hex)) (fn (a) string))))

(defn main () (println (hex 255)))
```

## Fixes

Drop both `requires` clauses, because Rust's `LowerHex` and `UpperHex` require
no other trait:

```lisp
(rust-trait lower-hex "std::fmt::LowerHex" i64)

(rust-trait upper-hex "std::fmt::UpperHex" i64)

(def hex
  (rust "format!(\"{{:x}}\", {0})"
    (where ((a lower-hex)) (fn (a) string))))

(defn main () (println (hex 255)))
```
