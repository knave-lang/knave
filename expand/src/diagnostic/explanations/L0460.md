A pattern labels a field that its type does not have. In a struct pattern, or in
the pattern of a variant with named fields, the label names no field that the
type declares, such as `(point :z a)` or `(labeled :txt t)`. On a variant with a
positional payload or no payload, such as `(circle :radius r)` or `(dot :radius
r)`, any label reports it, because the payload has no field names. The rule
holds in a `match` arm, in `let-match`, and nested inside another pattern.

A call of a variant with a positional payload or no payload reports it too when
its argument count differs from the number of parts and its arguments are all
`:label value` pairs, such as `(shape/circle :radius 1.0)`. With exactly one
argument per part, a keyword stays a value.

A pattern checks its labels in the order written, so a label that names no field
reports this error when it comes before a label used twice or a label with no
pattern after it, and `(point :z)` reports it. A pattern that also holds a part
by position reports `L0462` first. A label that names no field in a construction
of a struct, or of a variant with named fields, reports `L0408` instead.

## Erroneous example

```lisp
(defstruct point :x i64 :y i64)

(defn main ()
  (match (->point :x 1 :y 2)
    ((point :x a :z b) (println a " " b))))
```

## Fixes

Match `:y` in place of `:z`:

```lisp
(defstruct point :x i64 :y i64)

(defn main ()
  (match (->point :x 1 :y 2)
    ((point :x a :y b) (println a " " b))))
```

Or give the fields by position:

```lisp
(defstruct point :x i64 :y i64)

(defn main ()
  (match (->point :x 1 :y 2)
    ((point a b) (println a " " b))))
```
