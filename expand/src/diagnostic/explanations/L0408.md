A field label or a field read names a field that the struct, or the enum variant
with named fields, does not declare. A label reports it in places such as a
construction with `->point` or `new`, an update with `assoc` or `update`, a
field step of `assoc-in`, or a variant built from `:field value` pairs. A read
such as `(:z p)` or `(field p z)` reports it when the type of `p` is known to be
the struct, directly or through borrows. Labels are checked in the order
written, before the check for fields that a construction leaves out.

A read or an update on a value whose type is still unknown reports `L0407`
instead, a label in a pattern or on a variant with a positional payload reports
`L0460`, a label in a `defaults` clause reports `L0324`, and a label given twice
reports `L0454`.

## Erroneous example

```knave
(defstruct point :x i64 :y i64)

(defn main ()
  (let (p (->point :x 1 :z 2))
    (println (:x p))))
```

## Fixes

Use a field that the struct declares:

```knave
(defstruct point :x i64 :y i64)

(defn main ()
  (let (p (->point :x 1 :y 2))
    (println (:x p))))
```

Or declare the field in the struct, and give every field a value:

```knave
(defstruct point :x i64 :y i64 :z i64)

(defn main ()
  (let (p (->point :x 1 :y 2 :z 2))
    (println (:x p))))
```
